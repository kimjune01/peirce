In the syllogism in {del}Barbara{/del} {add}Barbara{/add},
        Every S is M; Every [unclear: M?] is a P;
        Hence, Every S is a P;
the minor term, S, occurs only as subject,
the major term, P, only as predicate. It
is, therefore, evident that since P may
be any term whatever, it may be either
affirmative or negative, and since S may
be any term whatever, it may be either
universal or particular. We, thus, have
three other moods of syllogism which with
Barbara make up the four Aristotelian
moods of the first figure of Syllogism.
