                 Harvard University,

Committee of the Carnegie Institute,
        Gentlemen:

                 I have just learned with extreme regret
that the application of Dr. Charles Saunders Peirce for as-
sistance by the Institute in the execution of his plan of
philosophic work has been rejected.
        I beg to submit an urgent recommendation for a revision
of the judgment in this case.  Some few years ago, in common
with many others, I looked forward to the completion of a
series of works outlined by Dr. Peirce which he proposed to
publish by subscription, and which from one of his remarkable
attainments in logic and philosophy promised to be amongst
the most notable of American productions in these fields,and
also much the largest single systematic achievement by any
American.  The plan failed from want of support -- in my
judgment, to the very considerable loss of philosophy.  The
learning, subtlety, originality and boldness of his sugges-
tions in fundamental philosophy place him among the foremost
thinkers in this country in his chosen subjects.  His work,in
the Algebra of logic, has been treated with great considera-
tion by important Germany writers.  I am convinced that a
highly valuable talent will be passed by and a great scientif
ic work discouraged, if all support should be withheld from
his undertaking.
                         Very respectfully yours,
                              Dickinson S. Miller.

January 3, 1903.
