honor to address your Committee, and to say that I have in my hands
certain manuscripts, embodying the results of some of Mr. Peirce's phil-
osophical investigations, which I am prepared to submit to your Committee
on his behalf, under Article VI of the purposes which in his Trust Deed
Mr. Carnegie declared to be among the aims of his foundation, viz:
"To ensure the prompt publication and distribution of the results of
scientific investigation, a field highly important."

        These manuscripts do not, however, form any part of the projected
work, embodying the final and complete result of Mr. Peirce's investiga-
tions in the entire field of logic and philosophy, for the preparation of
which I have the honor to urge your Committee to make provision.

        Should the Carnegie Institution find itself justified in taking up
the publication of the manuscripts now in my hands, paying to Mr. Peirce
a suitable honorarium therefor, he would then be in a position to commence
his greater work under the auspices of the Carnegie Institution, in a
series of memoirs under such a plan as that proposed by Professor Cattell
in his letter to your Committee, or such other adequate plan as to you
may seem most suitable to the attainment of the object.

        I have the honor to be, Gentlemen,
