No. 10. On the Presuppositions of Logic.
    I here show that much that is generally set down as pre-
supposed in logic is neither needed nor warranted.  The true
presuppositions of logic are merely hopes; and as such, when
we consider their consequences collectively, we cannot condemn
scepticism as to how far they may be borne out by facts.  But
when we come down to specific cases, these hopes are so com-
pletely justified that the smallest conflict with them suffices
to condemn the doctrine that involves that conflict.  This is
one of the places where logic comes in contact with ethics.
I examine the matter of these hopes, showing that they are,
