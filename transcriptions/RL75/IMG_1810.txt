        Many years ago I published a little paper on the Economy
of Research, in which I considered this problem.  Somebody furnished
a fund to be expended upon research without restrictions.  What sort
of researches should it be expended upon?  My answer, to which I still
adhere, was this.  Researches for which men have been trained, instru-
ments procured, and a plant established, should be continued while these
conditions subsist.  But the new money should mainly go to opening
up new fields; because new fields will probably be more profitable,
and, at any rate, will be profitable longer.
