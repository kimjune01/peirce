Chicago, August 1, 1902.

To the Executive Committee
        of the Carnegie Institution,
                Washington.

Gentlemen:

        You have, as I understand from Dr. C.S. Peirce, under con-
sideration a proposition from him relating to a series of memoirs on
logic.  May I be permitted to testify to the very high position which
his work on the logic of relations holds in the estimation of mathemati-
cians and to express the hope that the Carnegie Institution will find it
desirable to arrange to make it possible for him to devote his energies
to work leading to publication in the domain of logic ?

        Believe me, with the highest respect,

                Yours very truly,

                        (signed) E.H. Moore.
