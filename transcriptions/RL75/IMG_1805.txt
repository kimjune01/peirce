        No. 28.   On the Economics of Research.

          In all economics the laws are ideal formulae from which
there are large deviations, even statistically.     In the economics
of research the "laws" are mere general tendencies to which excep-
tions are frequent.     The laws being so indefinite, at best, there is
little advantage in very accurate definitions of such terms as
"amount of knowledge".     It is, however, possible to attach a definite
conception to one increment of knowledge being greater than another.
