OFFICE OF
THIRD ASSISTANT SECRETARY.

                              DEPARTMENT OF STATE,
                                  WASHINGTON,

                                                        January 9, 1903.

My dear Charlie:

                 I have your letter of January 7th. and have carefully

noted what you have to say.  I now feel hopeful of eventual success in

this matter, but we must make what arrangement we can, and only hope that

the limitations may not be such as to impair the value of your work.

The plan suggested by Professor Cattell is that a Committee should be ap-

pointed on Logic and Scientific methods, consisting of Royce of Harvard,

Dewey of Chicago, and Fullerton of Pennsylvania.  At least two of these

have written very favorably of you and have shown themselves to be warm

advocates of my plan, and I feel sure that you could work under such a

Committee.  The proposition is that they should supervise the work and

defray expenses as it proceeds, and this plan seems to meet the views of

the Executive Committee,- so far as I have been able to ascertain.  To
