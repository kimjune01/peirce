                    DIVISION OF
        PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND ANTHROPOLOGY


            Garrison-on-Hudson,  N.Y.  January 22, 1903



Dear Mr. Peirce:-
        I have read with much interest the copy of your ap-
plication to the executive committee of the Carnegie Institution,
which Dr. Jacobi handed to me.    I understand that the matter
is still under advisement by the committee.    I have written
urging as strongly as I can favorable action, as I regard the
publication of your work as of the utmost importance to the
world.
        I have spoken to President Butler in regard to the
possibility of an appointment at this university, but he does
not regard it as feasible.
        Trusting that through the Carnegie Institution you
may be given the opportunity of completing your work under
favorable conditions,
                                      Very truly yours,
                                          J, McKeen Cattell


Mr. C.S. Peirce,
        Milford, Pa.


Dictated.    Signed in Mr. Cattell's absence.
