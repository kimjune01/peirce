                               Cambridge, December 31, 1902.

My Dear Sir:
        Some friends of my brother Charles who believe that it is
desirable to bring about the publication of the results of his life-
long studies in Logic and Philosophy are presenting the case to the
executive committee of the Carnegie Institution as one worthy of
their favorable consideration.  My brother has been a profound stu-
dent in this field ever since he was a boy in college.  He is ac-
knowledged in Europe as well as in America to be one of the first
living authorities on the subject of Logic, and is everywhere known
as a man of a high order of intellectual power.  His friends and
fellow students feel assured that he has reached views which it is
of real importance to the world, in the interests of learning, to
have fully set forth by the hand of their author.  But he is not
in a position, without some aid, to devote the necessary two or
three years to the labor of preparing a book which could never be
remunerative.  It is believed, therefore, that an appeal may suit-
ably be made, and with considerable force, to the trustees of the
Carnegie fund, of which a leading object is to encourage original
research in all branches of human inquiry.
        Such an appeal has already been presented, and has the
support of metaphysicians of high reputation, such, for example, as
Professors William James and Josiah Royce, who have manifested a
warm interest in it.  A more general support would, however, be a
