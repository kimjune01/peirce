28. On Clearness of Ideas. In 1877 I published a paper on this
subject in which I set forth a doctrine called pragmatism,
which has since been talked of. But I know more about the
clearness of ideas than I did a quarter of a century ago. I there
described three grades of clearness, 1st, that which results
from familiar use of the conception; 2nd, that which results
from logical analysis and is expressed by a formal definition;
and 3rd, that which results from understanding the practical
implication of the conception. I propose in this memoir to
