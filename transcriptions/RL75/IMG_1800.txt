        In this paper, besides very important improvements in the
subdivision of the three kinds of simple arguments, with several
hitherto unrecognised types, and far greater clearness of exposition,
I shall have much that is new to say about mixed arguments, which pre-
sent man[hand: y] points of importance and of interest that have never been
remarked.  I shall give a new classification of them based, not upon
the nature of their elements, but upon their modes of combination.
Besides setting forth my own doctrine of the stechiology of argument,
I shall examine the most important of those which are opposed to it.

     No. 20.    Of Critical Logic in General.

     No. 21.    Of First Premisses.

        My position on this subject comes under the general head of
sensationalism; but I contend that criticism is inapplicable to what
