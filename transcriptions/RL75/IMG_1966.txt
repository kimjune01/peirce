No. 11. On the Logical Conception of Mind.
        This memoir is here placed, or perhaps better before
No. 9, for the sake of perspicuity of exposition.  The matter
of it will have to be somewhat transformed at a later stage.
If the logician is to talk of the operations of the mind at
all, as it is desirable that he should do, though it is not
scientifically indispensable, then he must mean by "mind"
something quite different from the object of study of the
psychologist; and this logical conception of mind is devel-
oped in this memoir and rendered clear.*
