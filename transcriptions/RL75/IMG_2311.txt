In any given inquiry, other things being fairly equal {del}to us{/del} even
considerably against equality, prefer the hypothesis which
if false can easily be proved to be so; if it can very easily be
dispatched, adopt it at once, and have done with it. But
while you hold it, hold it in good faith, so as to do it full
justice. Among hypotheses choose one whose elements are well
understood, so that unknown complications, and consequent
expense of energy cannot arise. Prefer general hypotheses to
special ones, provided the mere general are so by being
simpler; if they are so by being complex, it is necessary to
consider the economics of testing them more particularly.
For instance, instead of supposing y = a + bx + cx² + dx³ + etc.,
and determining the coefficients, ask whether y has a
constant term, next whether it tends to infinity with x,
next whether its increments are approximately propor-
tional to those of x, etc.
