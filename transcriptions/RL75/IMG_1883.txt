        Carnegie Institution
        WASHINGTON, D. C.

                                      Nov 29 1902

Dear Mr Peirce:

        I like a juicy letter
and that's the sort that always
comes from Milford. I can
only guess what will finally result
from your proposition about the
Logic. Personally I have convictions
on the subject and have frankly
spoken them to the Secretary.
The effect of this will be, I hope, to
hasten a decision though I can not
guess what the decision will be.
The Trustees have met and adjourned
after placing $200 000 at the disposition
of the Ex. Com. The Ex. Com. has
also met and awarded certain
grants under this power and it
has many more still pending. We
at the office are now at work on
the grist upon which we expect to
make a good deal of progress in the
next 2 or 3 weeks. Indeed I am
desirous of clearing up everything
that has been determined upon,
in that time.
        I think a letter has {del}all{/del} already
gone to you notifying that
your case has been for the time
defered. Like you, I too, believe
in a generalissimo.

        Heartily Yours

                Marcus Baker
