Beyond averring that conviction, I do not offer myself
as a witness to the utility of the work. I should, indeed, not
have gone so far as I have done, were I not persuaded
that the Executive Committee ought to require, as one of
the first conditions of extending aid to any work, that the
person who was to do it should be saturated with faith in
its utility and value.
I will indicate certain lines of thought, which, if pur-
sued by the Executive Committee, may determine an opinion
in regard to the utility of the work I propose. These lines of
thought are two. The one bears upon the value of my researches
considered as contributions to pure science; the other relates
to their probable influence, direct or indirect, upon the pro-
gress of other sciences. I will first venture upon a few sug-
gestions along the latter line.
