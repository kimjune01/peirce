Mr. Peirce has entrusted to my charge the matter of his applica-
tion to the Carnegie Institution, and it is in this capacity that I have
the honor to address your Committee, and to say that I have in my hands
certain manuscripts, embodying the results of some of Mr. Peirce's philos-
ophical investigations, which I am prepared to submit to your Committee
on his behalf, under Article 6 of the purposes which in his Trust Deed Mr.
Carnegie declared to be among the aims of his foundation, viz: "To ensure
the prompt publication and distribution of the results of scientific inves-
tigation, a field highly important".
     These manuscripts do not, however, form any part of the projected work,
embodying the final and complete results of Mr. Peirce's investigations in
the entire field of logic and philosophy, for the preparation of which I
have the honor to urge your Committee to make provision.
     Should the Carnegie Institution find itself justified in taking up
the publication of the manuscripts now in my hands, paying to Mr. Peirce a
suitable honorarium therefor, he would then be in a position to commence
his greater work under the auspices of the Carnegie Institution, in a se-
ries of memoirs under such a plan as that proposed by Professor Cattell in
his letter to your Committee, or such other adequate plan as to you may
seem most suitable to the attainment of the object.

               I have the honor to be, Gentlemen,

                    Your obedient servant,
