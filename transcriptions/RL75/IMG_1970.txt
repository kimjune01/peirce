which I correctly described, forgetting that according to my

Thus, the only error that paper contains is the designation
as Abduction of a mode of induction somewhat resembling abduc-
tion, which may properly be called abductive induction.  It was
this resemblance which deceived me, and subsequently led me
into a further error contrary to my own previous correct state-
ment.  Namely, continuing to confound Abduction and Abductive
Induction, in subsequent reflections upon the rationale of Ab-
duction, I was led to see that this rationale was not that
which I had in my Johns Hopkins paper given of Induction; and
in a statement I published in the Monist, I was led to give the
correct rationale of Abduction as applying to Abductive Induc-
tion and so, in fact, to all Induction.  All the difficulties
with which I labored are now completely disposed of by recog-
nizing that Abductive Induction is quite a different thing from
Abduction.  It is a very instructive illustration both of the
dangers and of the strength of my heuretic method.  Similar
errors may remain in my system.  I shall be very thankful to
