I.

That which I desire aid in doing is to bring before the world
the results of my researches into logic.
I began this study in 1856; and it has been my principal
occupation ever since. I cannot lay claim to the slightest
merit for the constancy with which I have pursued it, since
it has been an uncontrollable impulse. On the contrary, it
has been necessary for me at all times to exercise all my
control over myself, for fear {del}of{/del} that my mind might be
affected by such unceasing application to a particular
subject. When I have found myself in a solitary situation, and
{del}there was not a daily round of duties to occupy me,{/del}, I have had
desperate struggles with my logic. It has kept me poor; but
