quite a revolution in the study of exact Logic.  Upon his results
Schroeder's monumental, and still unfinished, Algebra der Logik is
founded.  Mr. Peirce's theory of induction,- still another of
his topics of research,- is original and highly important.  Few
men have the mastery that he possesses over the wide field of
the comparative and historical study of the methods of various
sciences.  He unites in his work the training and the exactness
of a mathematician with the speculative ingenuity of a really
great philosopher.  It will be a great loss to the world if those
of his researches which have not yet been put into final shape,
do not receive the finishing touches, and are not published.
Had I the means, I should cheerfully pay the cost of securing the
completion and publication of what I still hope to see him do,
and should deem a considerable personal sacrifice for such an
end well repaid by the scientific and philosophical results that
I should confidently expect to receive from him.
        From the point of view of the interests of Logic, of
Philosophy in general, and of all students of general science,
I am therefore sure that Mr. Peirce's researches in the mention-
ed fields are thoroughly worthy of whatever endowment is neces-
sary to secure the effective revision, the final preparation, and
the publication, of his still incomplete work in Logic, whether
it be printed as one whole, or in separate memoirs.
