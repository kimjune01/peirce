No. 23.  On the Validity of Induction.

No. 24.  On the Justification of Abduction.  The categories
[hand: bracket] furnish the definition of abduction, from which follows its mode
of justification, and from this again its rules.  The various
maxims which are found in different books are passed in review
and, for the most part, are found to sin only in vagueness.  One
question not very commonly studied is what is the character of
a phenomenon which makes it call for explanation.  The theory
of Dr. Carus that it is irregularity, and that of Mr. Venn that
it is isolation, though the latter is defended with some power,
are positively refuted.  This refutation does not apply to the
theory that the character sought is that of being surprising.
This, however, is open to another kind of objection.  The true
doctrine is nearly this, however.
